---
title: "Evaluation must outlive the reward function"
---

A model can optimize the evaluator's shortcuts rather than the desired behavior. Reward and held-out evaluation should therefore be related but independently audited. Our clinical case makes this visible: formatting a probability correctly is easy to verify, while establishing valid prognosis is much harder.

## Decompose the task

Track record fidelity, information availability, predictive quality, calibration, abstention, cost, and robustness separately. Do not collapse them into one scalar before understanding their failure modes. A tool call that succeeds only confirms execution; it does not deserve a clinical-quality reward by itself.

For a fully observed binary endpoint, Brier score is (p-y)^2. Censored outcomes need an explicit estimand and an appropriate handling method; pretending a censored patient is a negative label is invalid. Our existing outcome lesson discusses censoring, and this experiment only uses invented fully observed labels.

## A scalar can hide a trade-off

The example keeps quality and cost visible before combining them. Lambda is a pedagogical choice, not a clinical recommendation.

```python
import torch
p = torch.tensor([0.8, 0.6, 0.4])
y = torch.tensor([1.0, 1.0, 0.0])
queries = torch.tensor([2.0, 5.0, 1.0])
quality = 1 - (p-y).square()
reward = quality - 0.02 * queries
print("quality:", [round(v, 4) for v in quality.tolist()])
print("query cost:", [round(v, 4) for v in (0.02*queries).tolist()])
print("combined reward:", [round(v, 4) for v in reward.tolist()])
```

Output:

```text
quality: [0.96, 0.84, 0.84]
query cost: [0.04, 0.1, 0.02]
combined reward: [0.92, 0.74, 0.82]
```

## Compare at matched budgets

Measure one-sample performance separately from best-of-G or pass@G. More attempts can improve success without any parameter update. Match generation tokens, teacher calls, tools, and evaluation procedures when comparing training algorithms. Report unique task count, repeated rollout count, and uncertainty at the patient/task level rather than treating correlated rollouts as independent patients.

For records, test availability filtering, patient isolation, contradictory revisions, missing values, and unit conversions. For behavior, test repeated queries, malformed arguments, fabricated citations, and exhaustion. Use adversarial cases independent of training reward tuning. Clinical deployment requires real-data evaluation beyond these synthetic fixtures.
